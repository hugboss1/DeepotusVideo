You will be able to prepare a 3D part before sending it to the slicer: scale it, repair it, lay it down, check its overhangs, hollow it, arrange it on the bed, then export it to the printer or a game engine. Free and local. Nothing is irreversible: every gesture that changes the part writes one more version, and all of them stay on disk.

Open the Workbench

In the 3D Studio, click the 07 · 3D Workbench node; the Workbench takes over the page, and 3D Studio at the top left brings you back. On the left, the model's life lists each model and its versions: one click loads a version, Alt+click compares it (A/B view). On the right, four tabs: Parts, Rig, Report, Export. Help opens this chapter's step-by-step and glossary.

The main gesture

  1. Set a target size. target size field: the largest wanted dimension, in millimetres (80 for a figurine, 250 for a board). A 3D file carries no unit: while this field is empty the Workbench shows “glTF units” and refuses whatever needs a physical dimension (hollowing, arranging, exporting for print). A refusal beats an invented millimetre.
  2. Pick the printer. The Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2 profile is built in (256 × 256 mm bed, 256 mm height, one excluded area); installed OrcaSlicer or ElegooSlicer profiles show up too, read, never modified, and you can enter one by hand. On the plate, the green outline is the real bed, the red rectangle the area where nothing may sit.
  3. Repair. Report tab, Repair in one click: coincident vertices welded, duplicate faces and flat triangles dropped, normals unified. Capping holes is unchecked by default: it adds material. The detail appears in the bottom bar.
  4. Lay the part flat. Place on a face (F), then click the face that must touch the bed. The gesture joins the queue, and write the version saves it. If unsure, Orient proposes three ranked poses (support, overhang, height) in the Report tab. A computed orientation does not always find the best pose: always review the proposal, no score knows which face you want to look good. If the queue holds both a placement and a recentring, the bar flags the contradiction and greys out writing.
  5. Look at the overhangs, then the slices (see below).
  6. Hollow it, when it helps. Report, Hollow, 2 mm wall to begin with: the solid inside becomes a shell. It is first a resin (SLA) gesture; with filament (FDM), the slicer already fills the inside. It needs a closed mesh, and the Workbench states the thickness that holds when the requested wall is too thick. For resin, then drill a drain hole: Drill (drain hole), 3 to 4 mm, then click the face, preferably near the bed. The drill goes through the one wall under the click and stitches the hole closed.
  7. Arrange on the bed, measure, then export (see below).

The order that works: repair, lay down, inspect, hollow, export. Repair first, because every other gesture starts from sound geometry, and hollowing refuses an open mesh.

Look before slicing

Overhangs paints orange the faces leaning less than 45° from horizontal: there, a layer has almost nothing under it and the slicer will add a support. Re-lay the part until the orange recedes. Slices draws twenty cross-sections in blue to see the real shape at each height. Both are indicative previews: nothing is written, and the slicer does the real slicing.

The Report tab

Decimate, extract

Decimate reduces triangles towards a target and says if it is not reached; extracting one by one splits each element into a sibling version.

Materials

Colour, opacity, metal, roughness, emission, alpha mode and double side, with preview; only the touched fields are written.

Measure

Measure: two clicks give a distance, its x/y/z components and the angle between the two faces, drawn in the scene.

Parts, booleans, connectors

Booleans

Parts tab: A = selection, B = selection, then union, difference (A − B) or intersection and Apply. The bar states the seam and lost materials.

Cut and assemble

The Knife cuts the part; its bar adds a connector: dowel (holes and a separate pin), plug and its socket with clearance, or a sliding dovetail.

Skin

A Material Forge material on the ticked parts: Skin. Masks shows cavities (red) and edges (green) in view B.

The Rig tab draws the skeleton through the skin, highlights a bone's chain, colours its influence weights, rotates a bone with three x/y/z sliders and plays the shipped animation clips.

Arrange on the bed

On the plate separates the pieces, then Arrange on the bed lays them inside the real bed, rotated when that saves room, 2 mm apart, on up to eight beds; what does not fit is reported. Drag a piece (snapped to the bed grid, Shift frees it), nudge it with the arrow keys: its position, size and angle are shown. The plate is a view: it is saved, but it does not change the model.

Export

  1. Export tab, 3D printing: the STL and 3MF of the displayed version, at the target size in millimetres (see 3D-print your creations). Open in the slicer takes you there.
  2. For an engine: Prepare writes one glTF per target (Blender, Godot, Unreal, Unity) with its import sheet; dropping copies it into the engine project; Open launches Blender or the project; Back from Blender re-imports a fixed GLB as one more version and says what was lost.

Glossary

The words your slicer will use, explained once.

TermWhat it is
Base (bed contact)The face that touches the bed. Wide and flat, the print holds; narrow, it comes loose.
OverhangA wall leaning too far to rest on the layer below. Prusa puts the clean limit without support between 45 and 60° from horizontal.
SupportA disposable structure holding overhangs up. The slicer generates it; the Workbench only helps you need less of it.
BrimA one-layer collar around the part, to keep its corners from lifting.
RaftA platform printed UNDER the whole part. Safer than a brim, costlier, and it leaves a rough face.
SkirtAn outline drawn next to the part, not touching it, to prime the flow before starting.
InfillThe pattern inside. Gyroid is about equally strong in every direction; Lightning only supports top surfaces. 15 % is usually enough.
SeamEach wall loop starts and ends somewhere, leaving a visible vertical line. The slicer places it (Nearest, Aligned, Rear) or scatters it (Random).
RetractionFilament pulled back during travel moves, to avoid strings (stringing).
LayerA horizontal slice. 0.2 mm is the usual compromise; 0.12 mm for detail, 0.28 mm for speed.
Perimeter (wall)The number of wall loops. Two perimeters at 0.45 mm make a 0.9 mm wall: the minimum to aim for when modeling.
BridgingPrinting a layer over empty space, between two supports. It works over a few centimeters, with good cooling.
WarpingCorners lifting as they cool. Remedies: clean bed, brim, closed enclosure. Common with ABS/ASA, rare with PLA.
WatertightnessA solid is watertight when its surface is closed, with no hole or free edge. That is what the Workbench bar calls "fermé" (closed).
ManifoldEvery edge belongs to exactly two faces. A mesh can be closed and still be hard to slice cleanly if it is not.
DecimationReducing the triangle count. A 500,000-triangle mesh prints no better than a 100,000 one, but slows everything down. Report tab › Decimate.
HollowingReplacing the solid inside with a shell of given thickness. Mostly useful in resin; 2 mm is a good starting point.
Drain holeThe hole(s) letting uncured resin (and air) out of a hollowed part. In the Workbench: Report tab › Drill (drain hole).
