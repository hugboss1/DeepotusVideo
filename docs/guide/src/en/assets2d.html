You will be able to find your way around the Game Assets hub, turn an image into a 3D model and open the right 2D tool: Sprite Lab, Tile Lab, Vectorlab's Pixel persona or Card Forge. The 2D labs are free and run on your PC; 3D generation is paid, price shown first.

The Game Assets hub

Open Game Assets in the rail. One tab per tool: 3D (image to 3D model), 3D Studio (see Image to 3D), Assets 2D, Sprites 2D and Tiles (the labs opened inside the hub), Materials (Material Forge) and Cards (Card Forge).

An image becomes a 3D model

  1. In the 3D tab, pick a source image from the Library: ideally a character or object cleanly cut out on a neutral background.
  2. Pick the engine, from cheapest to most refined: TripoSR, Hunyuan3D, Tripo (default, good value), TRELLIS and Rodin. Only the fal.ai key is needed.
  3. Options: textures, T-pose for a character to animate, multi-view (four views generated first for a more faithful model), and formats: GLB always, plus FBX, OBJ, STL or USDZ depending on the engine.
  4. The total price is shown next to Generate 3D, before the run. The pipeline then shows each step live; the model turns in the viewer, every format and every view can be downloaded.

Models also land in the 3D tab of the Library; the Workbench then prepares them for printing.

Open a 2D tool

The Assets 2D tab gathers four cards, each with Open and Guide:

Sprite Lab

An image, a video or a sheet becomes an animated sprite sheet, with directions, skeleton, hitboxes and engine exports. See Animate sprites.

Tile Lab

Seamless tiles, blob 47 or 16 tilesets, variants, iso and hex shapes, painted maps, exports to Tiled, LDtk and Godot. See Paint tile scenery.

Pixel — Vectorlab

Opens Vectorlab straight in the Pixel persona: frame timeline, 2:1 iso tile, rasterize an image. See Pixel art.

Card Forge

Playing cards: printer templates, back and registration, languages, batch art, booklet. See Card Forge.

Shared palettes

Sprite Lab, Tile Lab and Vectorlab's Pixel persona share the same list of palettes: adaptive (N colours), PICO-8, Game Boy, NES, Sweetie 16, 1-bit, Grayscale 16 (sixteen greys) and Handheld 4 (four tones, handheld console style). A palette gives the same result in all three tools. Dithering (ordered or Floyd-Steinberg) softens gradients.

Good to know

Only 3D generation costs money: the price shown counts each extra view and format, and goes through your spending caps. The 2D labs are free.

3D rendering runs in your browser: keep the tab in front for smooth rotation. Files live in the Deepotus data folder and follow a transfer.
