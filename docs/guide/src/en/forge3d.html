You will be able to turn a card into a printable 3D piece with Card Forge's 3D Forge, and produce a complete 3D model (mesh, texture, skeleton, animations, formats) in the 3D Studio. Local pipelines are free; image-to-3D engines are paid, price shown before any run.

From card to object: the 3D Forge

  1. In Card Forge, open module 09 3D Forge then Export layers: background, illustration, veil, frame, typography, ornaments and composite, front and back. The screen proves the stacking (0 px gap) before delivering, and the server checks it again.
  2. Edit the graph: each layer gets a flat or relief treatment (closed solid), a Material Forge material tiled at physical scale, a finish (holographic silver or gold, lacquer, leather, brushed metal, emissive) and a placement. A relief with no depth entered takes its material's physical height, otherwise 0.6 mm.
  3. The “3D mesh” treatment sends a layer to an image-to-3D engine: the price shows on the node and at the foot of the graph before any run.
  4. Build the 3D artifact assembles the final GLB, and an STL if every element is a closed solid; otherwise the refusal says why. The manifest lists elements, engines and credits.

The “Game objects” panel also creates printable 3D pieces: a Token, a Card relief token, a pawn or a stand, plus the Unfolded box (PDF)…, the vector pattern of a tuck box at the deck's thickness.

The 3D Studio

The 3D Studio (a sub-tab of the Game Assets hub) now takes a model from start to finish, screen by screen: Prepare, Texture, Rig, Refine, Animate, Export and Scenery (see The 3D Studio, screen by screen). Yesterday's Meshy graph remains as the Graph screen: from prompt or start image to deliverables, each node carries its cost and points to the screen that took over its function. The series cost shows before Run, and the log follows each task. A Meshy key is needed; fal engines (tripo, rodin, hunyuan, trellis, triposr) use your fal key.

Prepare the views before paying for the mesh

  1. In the hub's 3D card, the “Views first” block generates the views first (front, back, profiles): a failed view does not cost a mesh.
  2. For free, reuse the views of a bible character's sheet, or build the view set from your photos (phone ones first, straightened and cut out).
  3. Per thumbnail: Remove the background (local, free) or replay a single view with a corrected prompt (paid).
  4. “Run · engine” launches the mesh on the approved views: only the engine is billed.

Rig, conversion, optimisation

fal workshop

fal workshop (rig a job) lists your 3D jobs: Meshy rig with animations (walk, run…), quote per task; greyed buttons say why (key, approval, texture).

Convert and import

Free local conversion to OBJ, STL, 3MF or glTF (STL and 3MF at a size in mm), or by Meshy to FBX, USDZ, BLEND. Import a model (OBJ, STL, glTF, GLB) makes it a job.

LOD and textures

The Optimise area derives levels of detail with measured loss and mobile, PC, print budgets, and exports textures to Blender, Unity, Unreal or Godot conventions.

Engine bench

The medians measured on your machine per engine (triangles, weight, watertightness, cost); add an existing job to it for free. An optional local GPU service makes Hunyuan free.

Good to know

Paid: image-to-3D engines (fal in dollars, Meshy in credits), rigging, Meshy conversion and generated views. The price is announced first, the spending table prices rig and refine, and texturing without a fal key is refused straight away instead of failing later.
