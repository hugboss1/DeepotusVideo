You will be able to compose a complete card game in Card Forge, from the face to the printer-ready pack, and deliver it to a virtual table with its rulebook. Almost everything is free and local; only illustration generation and translation by a language model are paid, quote shown first.

The Card Forge screen

  1. Open the Cards tab of the Game Assets hub. Templates offers starter decks (New deck from this template).
  2. At the top, set the format (poker, bridge, tarot, mini, square, domino, business card, jumbo, micro), the resolution (150, 300 or 600 DPI), bleed, safe zone and corner radius. These settings apply to every part and to the export.
  3. On the left, eleven modules; in the middle, the card with its guides, front or back, card by card.

The eleven modules

01 Face · 02 Frame · 03 Typography

The illustration (imported, generated or taken from the local catalogue), borders, rarity and back, the title, rules and flavour text, with the shipped fonts.

04 Data

The deck comes from a CSV, a Notion export ZIP or a Google Sheets link…; each column feeds a field. The deck statistics (histograms, cost curve) and the “Back” line spot gaps.

05 Volume · 06 Materials

3D preview with real thickness and edge; eight PBR texture channels computed locally.

07 Print

Home sheets or printer templates, preflight, printer pack and duplex test chart (see below).

08 3D Export · 09 3D Forge

A GLB and a glTF with every PBR map; the 3D Forge turns the card into a printable object (see the 3D object).

10 Import · 11 Publishing

Import existing cards; deliver around the card: virtual tables, rulebook, mockup, product sheet.

Edit this face in the Vectorlab opens the rendered face in a Vectorlab document at the deck's physical size, with guides and a locked layer.

Several languages

Columns suffixed by language (name_fr, name_en…, twelve languages) are recognised: pick the active language and every part follows, with what is missing card by card. Translating a column with a language model is proposed card by card; nothing enters the table without your click, and the quote shows before any call (local Ollama: free).

Print

  1. Module 07: choose home imposition (A4 or Letter sheets, crop lines or crosses) or the exact template of MakePlayingCards, The Game Crafter or DriveThruCards (bleed, safe zone, DPI, pixels expected by the portal). An unverified format is greyed out and says so.
  2. Preflight re-reads the produced file itself: bleed, crop marks, the PDF's TrimBox and BleedBox, PNG density. Re-check runs it again.
  3. Printer pack (.zip) delivers named fronts and backs, padded to the deck size, with a manifest ready to upload. For DriveThruCards, the PDF becomes X-1a (CMYK) if the press profile is loaded; otherwise the gap is written on screen.
  4. Duplex test chart (PDF) prints two pages with a vernier to a tenth of a millimetre to measure your printer's front-back offset.

Publish and deliver

Virtual tables

Export for Tabletop Simulator (.zip) (10 × 7 sheets and saved object) or Export for Tabletopia (.zip) (one image per face). Place in Tabletop Simulator copies the deck into your saved objects.

Rulebook

Composed at 300 DPI with the app's fonts (“#” headings) and a card sheet.

Mockup and sheet

Cards fanned out (five at most) in square, story or landscape format, and a product sheet with figures, ready to copy.

Deck art can also be generated in a batch: a template prompt with {columns}, a style, a bible entity; quote per row, cap per batch and your spending caps are checked before the run.

Good to know

Paid: generating a face or the deck art, translating with an online model, and the 3D Forge's image-to-3D engines. Everything else is free and runs on your PC.
