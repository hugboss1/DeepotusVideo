Get started

Take a tour of the app#

You will find your way around the app, move from one screen to another without searching, and read what the top bar tells you. Five minutes, free.

Deepotus Video Gen is a studio that runs on your own computer. It turns an idea into a video, a picture, a 3D object or a published post. You plug in your own AI provider keys: you pay those providers, not the app. Everything you create stays with you.

The screen at a glance#

  1. The rail, on the left: one screen per task. Quick for a one-click video, Studio to compose, Edit to cut a video, Library to find your media, Settings for the keys. The arrow at the top of the rail folds it into icons only.
  2. The quick command: click Quick command… or press Ctrl+K, type a few letters, Enter.
  3. The spending counter shows what you spent this month with your providers. One click opens Settings.
  4. The badges fal, heygen and voice turn green when the matching key is set. One click takes you to that key.
  5. The render queue, top right, lists what is being computed and what just finished, in the Running and Recent tabs.

Go fast with the quick command#

The quick command goes to any screen (Go to Studio, Go to News…), opens the last render, creates a starter graph or opens a Settings section. Replay onboarding restarts the first-launch tour.

What the app does to protect you#

Nothing leaves without you

Only this PC can change, generate or spend. Another device on the network can only read, and only once paired.

Errors that make sense

A missing key or a server refusal is shown in plain words, with what to do.

Safe imports

A picture with the same name becomes name-1, name-2…; a file that is not a real picture is refused and named.

In-app dialogs

Confirmations and text prompts use one Deepotus dialog, never a browser pop-up.

Good to know#

The desktop icon opens the app at http://deepotus.localhost:8765. Each browser tab carries the name of the open tool, for example “Deepotus — Vectorlab”.

Only AI generations cost money, and your provider bills them. The chapter Keep spending under control shows how to set limits.

Install, pick a language, update#

You will launch the app for the first time, pick its language, know what each use costs and keep it up to date. Ten minutes, free.

First launch#

  1. During installation, pick the language: French or English. It becomes the language of the whole app.
  2. Double-click the Deepotus Video Gen desktop icon. The app starts without a black window, then your browser opens on the studio.
  3. On the very first launch, a five-step welcome introduces the studio and lets you pick a persona, your generation providers and the networks you publish to.
  4. Set at least one key, the fal.ai one: it makes pictures and video clips. The chapter Connect your keys shows how.

The welcome can be replayed at any time from the quick command (Ctrl+K): Replay onboarding.

Switch language#

  1. Open Settings › Provider defaults.
  2. In Interface language, pick Français or English. The page reloads in the chosen language.

Translated: the sidebar, Settings, the Library, Quick, News, the Scheduler, the Studio, Templates, Episodes, the editor, Sound & VFX, subtitles, machine transfer and every confirmation dialog. Lists sent by the server (effects, transitions, presets) also arrive in the chosen language. The labs (Vectorlab, Photolab, Card Forge…) are being translated one by one; meanwhile their known texts are translated on display. What you type yourself is never translated.

Keep the app up to date#

  1. Once a day, the app checks whether a new version is published. It never blocks anything.
  2. When there is one, a card announces it: Download fetches the installer and shows where it is saved, Later hides the card for that version, Notes tells what changes.
  3. To check right now: Settings › Diagnostics, Check button.
  4. Close the app, run the downloaded installer: nothing is installed on its own.

Connect your keys and AI engines#

You will set an AI provider's key, choose which engine does what, and protect your keys with a password. Ten minutes, free; keys are created with each provider.

Before you start#

An API key is a code the provider gives you on its website, in your account. It lets the app work on your behalf; the provider bills you. Only one is required to start: fal.ai.

Set a key#

  1. Open Settings › API keys. Each provider has its row and its status: missing until a key is set.
  2. Click Guide: it tells where to create the key with the provider and how it is billed.
  3. Paste the key in the field, then Save. It works right away, no restart.
  4. Click Test on the row: the provider says whether it accepts the key, without spending anything.

To set several keys at once, fill in the fields then Save all changes.

In Settings › Provider defaults, each role has its engine: picture generation, voice, picture to video, avatar, voiceover, news summary, post planning. Only keys actually set are offered.

Protect your keys in the vault#

  1. Open Settings › Vault.
  2. Choose a master password (at least 8 characters), type it twice, then Set up vault. Your keys are encrypted and leave the plain-text file.
  3. Close removes the keys from memory: nothing paid can go out. Open makes them active again with the password.
  4. Remember on this PC opens the vault on its own at launch, for your Windows session; Stop remembering asks for the password again.

On each row of API keys, a badge tells where the key lives: in the file, in the vault, or vault closed (one click takes you there).

Good to know#

The vault password is stored nowhere: if lost, it cannot be recovered. Write it down, or keep an encrypted archive.

Export archive creates a .dzk file that carries your keys, caps and rates to another PC or your phone, protected by its own password. Import an archive… takes them back.

Settings › Diagnostics shows the status of all keys at once (never their value) and the providers' balances.

Keep spending under control#

You will know what you spend, set a cap per month and per engine, and decide yourself when a render may go over it. Five minutes, free.

Where to see what you spend#

The dollar counter in the top bar shows the month's total. One click opens Settings › Pricing & budget, where everything is set.

Set your rates and caps#

  1. At the top of Pricing & budget, enter your providers' rates (price of a picture, of a second of video, of a voice character…). The costs shown in Quick, the Studio and the Scheduler become accurate. Save pricing.
  2. Video cap per request limits the cost of a single video generation; Max generated seconds per clip limits its length.
  3. Further down, Monthly caps: one overall cap and one per engine (fal, OpenAI, Anthropic, ElevenLabs, HeyGen, Meshy), in dollars. A bar shows what was spent.
  4. Set Alert at (% of a cap): a banner warns you once a month when a cap reaches that level. Save caps.

When a render would go over a cap#

Before leaving, every paid generation is checked against your caps. If it goes over, a “Monthly spending cap” dialog opens, wherever you are in the app:

  1. “Tirer quand même” (run anyway) sends exactly the same request, once.
  2. “Annuler” (cancel) sends nothing and tells you why.

Actual versus estimated#

Below the caps, the Monthly spend — actual vs estimated table gives one row per engine and per screen: the number of runs, what was estimated, what was actually billed, and the difference.

Actual

The provider gave the exact price (Meshy, HeyGen): that is what counts.

Partly actual

Some runs have their exact price, the rest is still estimated.

Estimated

The provider gives no price: the app counts the quote made at launch.

Good to know#

Spending made from your phone goes into the same record and the same caps, counted once.

The Studio sends the cost it displays as the request's cap: a render cannot cost more than what you saw before clicking.

Move to another computer#

You will take all your work to another computer, check that nothing got damaged on the way, and get your keys back there. Fifteen minutes, free; the app must be installed on both machines.

What travels#

The transfer takes everything the app created: the Library and the origin of every file, renders, the bible, communication plans, vector documents, card series and 3D models. Your API keys never go into the package.

Export from the old PC#

  1. Open Settings › Machine transfer: the number of files and the size to take are shown.
  2. Click Export…, then pick the destination: a drive, a USB stick, or a path.
  3. Tick the extras if you want them: Logs and Discards — dated trash folders. They are unchecked by default, with their size.
  4. Start export: a dated folder is written at the destination, each file with its checksum. Do not close the app while it writes.
  5. Click Check integrity: every written file is read back and compared with its checksum.

Import on the new PC#

  1. Install the app, then open Settings › Machine transfer and click Import….
  2. Give the package folder, then Check: the app reads the package without writing anything and tells where it comes from (date, machine, version).
  3. Start import. Import adds: nothing this PC already has is overwritten. A damaged file is left out and named.

Take your keys too#

  1. On the old PC: Settings › Vault › Export archive, with a separate password.
  2. On the new PC: Import an archive… and that password. Your keys, caps and rates come back.

Good to know#

Paths from the old machine are rewritten for the new one: your projects find their files without any setting.

Stopping an export leaves an incomplete package. If it happens, run the export again, then Check integrity before importing.

Drive it from your phone#

You will link your phone to the PC, carry your posts and chapters in your pocket, and cut off a lost phone. Fifteen minutes; you need the Deepotus companion app on the phone and the same Wi-Fi.

Before you start#

By default, the app only listens on this PC: no other device can reach it. To open it to your home Wi-Fi, replace HOST=127.0.0.1 with HOST=0.0.0.0 in the .env file of the data folder, then restart the app. Windows will ask to allow Python through the firewall.

Pair the phone#

  1. Open Settings › Devices. The words this PC only or local network open tell whether the phone will be able to reach you.
  2. Click Pair a device: a QR code appears, valid for five minutes, for a single device.
  3. In the phone app, open Pair and scan the QR code. It closes on its own once read.
  4. To give your keys to the phone, export the encrypted archive from the Vault and open it in the phone app, with its password.

Five devices at most. A paired phone reads what the PC produces; it never sees your keys. It only writes to the PC to drop images and for Avatar live, whose spending goes through your caps.

The phone fetches the Library and its pictures, and resumes an interrupted download.

Send pictures to the PC

Your photos land in the Library with the “mobile” source, without duplicates.

The week's batch

Take the approved posts and publish them on Telegram or X from the phone. A post taken by the phone is no longer published by the PC.

Inbox

What you share to Deepotus lands here; pictures go on to the PC.

Offline chapters

Take a chapter, write without a network, hand it back to the PC.

Generate pictures

The cost is shown before each generation, at your PC's rates, and counted in your caps.

Avatar live

Film a take and start a Recast, or open a Live session: the PC does the work, the price is shown first (see Play an avatar).

A chapter taken to the phone#

While a chapter is on the phone, it is read-only on the PC, with a banner. You can force it back to the PC. If the two versions disagree, the phone's text stays in the log, to copy or take back: nothing is lost.

Remove a device#

  1. In Settings › Devices, click Revoke on the phone's row and confirm.
  2. Access is cut right away; the posts and chapters it had taken come back to the PC.

Good to know#

Lost your phone? Revoking is not enough: your keys travelled in the archive. Regenerate them with each provider; the Devices page gives the link to each console.

The phone is notified when a render finishes or fails, when a post is published or awaits an action, and when a cap is near.

Make videos

Make a video in minutes (Quick)#

You will make a vertical video in minutes: an animated clip from a picture, an avatar saying your text, both together, or a voiceover. Paid depending on the provider; the cost is shown before you click.

Before you start#

Quick has four tabs: Seedance (animated clip), HeyGen (talking avatar), Composition (avatar and clip together) and Voiceover. A tab stays greyed out while its provider's key is missing: fal.ai for Seedance, HeyGen for the avatar (chapter Connect your keys). The voiceover can also run for free on your PC with Voicebox.

Animate a picture into a clip#

  1. Open Quick, Seedance tab.
  2. Pick the start image: drop it on the area or click Browse thumbnails… to take it from the Library. An end image is optional; if the chosen model does not accept one, the field says so and names the models that do.
  3. Write what should move, or start from a prompt template, or click Open prompt generator.
  4. In Parameters, set the model, the duration and the format (9:16 by default).
  5. Read the estimated cost, then click the Generate button. The render goes through the render queue, top right, and lands in the Library.

With the default model, Seedance 2.5 at 720p, one second costs about $0.47: a 10 s clip comes to about $4.73. A shorter duration or another model costs less; the estimated cost updates with every setting.

Direct the camera#

  1. Unfold Camera (sliders): six sliders (push in, turn, rise…) become a sentence suited to the model, added to the prompt. It is shown before the render.
  2. To choose by eye, click Gallery: eleven camera moves in three styles, as small animations computed on your PC. It is a reminder, not a promise of what the model will do.

Make an avatar talk#

  1. HeyGen tab: pick an avatar and its voice. The lists show up right away, from a copy kept on your PC and refreshed in the background.
  2. For an avatar that looks like you, import a photo: it becomes a talking avatar, selected automatically.
  3. Write the script, then generate. The pauses in your text are kept.

Videos go through HeyGen and its Avatar III engine, picked according to the avatar. The HeyGen balance is shown in dollars. Count about $0.24 a minute at default rates.

An avatar and a clip in the same video, one after the other or side by side (vertical or horizontal split).

Voiceover

Music under the voice

When a render has both a voice and music, the music dips on its own under the voice.

Burned-in subtitles

Subtitle the render: aligning a text you provide is free; automatic transcription is paid and only runs if you tick it.

Extend a clip

From Send to, Extend adds about 7 generated seconds to a clip already rendered, with or without sound; the price is on each button.

Leaving Quick and coming back finds your settings as you left them.

For a still picture, use the Library: Create image, with the Prompt manager. For your own filmed video, Upload video in the Library, then the UGC video node of the Studio.

Compose with the Studio#

You will compose a video piece by piece by linking nodes, see it before paying, never pay twice for what is done, and reuse a graph like a recipe. Paid depending on the nodes; the graph's cost is shown before running.

The Studio screen#

On the left, the nodes palette (/ key) and the starter graphs. In the centre, the graph. On the right, the inspector of the selected node, which can be folded. At the top, the graph bar: New, Save, Recipe, Import, the format, Preview, the estimated cost and Run.

Compose a graph#

  1. Start from a starter graph (Seedance solo, Avatar post, UGC + animation, News reel post, Timeline montage) or from a blank page with New.
  2. Drag nodes from the palette: sources (picture, text, your video), generators (Seedance, HeyGen avatar, pictures), audio, editing, composition, output.
  3. Link an output, on the right of a node, to an input, on the left of another. Click a node to set it in the inspector.
  4. Preview computes the final composition for free: clips not generated yet are replaced by their picture.
  5. Read the estimated cost, then Run. That amount is sent as a cap: the render cannot cost more. Save keeps the graph under a name.

Never pay twice for what is done#

The paid render of a Seedance or HeyGen node is pinned automatically: as long as its request does not change, it is reused for free, and the estimated cost says which nodes are.

The last eight renders

The inspector lists the node's last renders; Use picks the one that feeds the rest.

Regenerate

For a new paid take despite the pin, explicitly ask for the node to be regenerated.

Reuse a graph as a recipe#

  1. Recipe freezes the graph: its pictures and texts become blanks to fill in.
  2. Run the recipe with new values: a quote is shown, you confirm. Unchanged nodes are reused for free.
  3. From a picture in the Library, Send to also offers to run a recipe on it.

Go further#

Engine duel

On a picture generation node, the same prompt goes to two models once you agree on the cost; you compare side by side and keep the winner.

Effects and masks

The Effects / Mask node, plugged into the Render's fx input: grades, VHS, colorisation…, on the whole render or a single layer.

AI fields

A field with a coloured edge is sent to an AI. Its badge shows the model and its price; its microphone dictates your text.

Voice and mix

The Voiceover node picks Voicebox or ElevenLabs; Audio mix dips the music under the voice (light, medium, strong).

Import a graph

Import opens a JSON graph; missing sources are listed and nothing is saved without your consent.

Frame by frame

The result drawer steps frame by frame with the , and . keys.

Good to know#

A video generation above the announced cost or your per-request cap is refused before any call. The generated length is capped in Settings; the rest is extended on your PC.

New Seedance nodes start with Seedance 2.5; a saved graph keeps its model. The default model is changed in Settings › Provider defaults.

If the browser cannot dictate, the recording can be transcribed by the server: it is paid, a quote is shown and “No” is the default choice.

Reuse templates and brand kits#

You will start from a ready-made layout, touch it up (slots, masks, texts, animations), adapt it to another format and dress it in your brand. Free; only renders of AI-generated slots cost money.

Pick a template#

Touch up a layout#

  3. Animation brings the slot in and out with a fade, slide or pop, with duration, delay and curve. Replay on canvas shows it.
  4. Add places a text, a ticker, a separator, the brand, a badge or a component.
  5. Save layout, then Open in Studio to fill it in and render it.

Make videos

Tell a story in episodes#

You will turn a text, a chapter or a novel into a narrated, illustrated video, scene by scene, and prepare its storyboard before paying for any render. Free up to narration and pictures; their cost is shown beforehand.

Two ways of working#

Open Chapters in the rail. The original flow (Episodes) goes straight from text to narrated video in four steps. The Chapter Workshop is the full space: manuscript, bible of characters and places, screenplay, storyboard, animatic.

An episode in four steps#

  1. Script and voice: give a title, paste the text or upload a .txt, .docx or .pdf. Pick the voice and language, then Generate narration: the whole chapter is read and stored in the audio Library.
  2. Scenes: split the text by paragraph (word for word) or by AI (into visual beats, with an illustration prompt). Edit, reorder, add or remove scenes.
  3. Illustrations: one picture per scene, with the model of your choice. For each scene, pick the animation: slow camera move (Ken Burns), real Seedance clip or still picture.
  4. Assembly and export: a quote gives the price of the Seedance scenes, then the episode is cut on its narration. If a Seedance scene fails, it falls back to a camera move and you are told.

At the top, New, Open and Save manage your episodes; a dot marks what is not saved. Narrate scenes never pays again for a scene already narrated with the same text, voice and language.

Install and train a local RVC voice#

You will learn to install Voixbox, record your voice, train it with RVC and have it speak during a Live session, on your own graphics card. Free: nothing goes to the Internet. Allow thirty minutes to install, a quarter of an hour to record and about an hour of computing per voice.

Before you start#

You need an NVIDIA graphics card with 6 GB of memory or more, and about 6 GB of disk. Without one, Live offers your voice as it is or the Character's voice through ElevenLabs (paid, nothing to install).

The engine is RVC (MIT license), through its official WebUI: the same install trains a voice and makes it speak. Only clone your own voice, or that of someone who has agreed to it.

Install Voixbox (once)#

  1. Open PowerShell in the app folder, where tools\voixbox lives (by default %LOCALAPPDATA%\DeepotusVideoGen).
  2. Run powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\voixbox\installer.ps1.
  3. The script installs uv, downloads a verified version of the WebUI, creates its Python, adds torch for CUDA (about 3 GB) then its weights (560 MB).
  4. It ends with CUDA True | NVIDIA …: the card is detected. On any failure, it stops and says why.

Record your voice#

The quality of the cloned voice depends almost entirely on this recording.

  1. Record 10 to 15 minutes of your voice alone: no music, no second voice, no echo.
  2. A quiet room, the mic 15-20 cm away, always the same mic. Speak the way you do on your live sessions, with varied sentences.
  3. Avoid clipping. Prefer several 30 s to 2 min files, in WAV.
  4. Put them in a folder that holds nothing else, for example C:\Voix\oli.

Train an RVC voice#

  1. Run powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\voixbox\webui.ps1. The WebUI opens in French at http://127.0.0.1:7865; keep the PowerShell window open.
  2. « Entraîner » (Train) tab, step 1: a short experiment name with no spaces or accents (oli), rate 40k, pitch guidance Oui (yes), version v2.
  3. Step 2: your recordings folder, and the rmvpe pitch algorithm.
  4. Step 3: save every 25 epochs, 200 epochs in total (100 for a first try), batch 8 (or 4 with 6 GB), small final model at each save: Oui (yes).
  5. Click « Entraînement en un clic » (one-click training). Allow about 1 h 15 for 200 epochs on an RTX 2080 Ti.
  6. Put the voice where Voixbox looks for it: powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\voixbox\ranger-voix.ps1 -Nom oli.

Listen to it before using it: in the WebUI's « Inférence du modèle » (model inference) tab, pick oli.pth and give it a short recording. A crackling voice needs more epochs or a cleaner recording.

Speak with it during a Live session#

  1. Run powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\voixbox\lancer.ps1 and keep the window open: it reports how many voices are trained.
  2. In Avatar live, Live tab, choose the local RVC voice under Voice sent.
  3. Set Transpose (semitones) if needed: +12 to go from a male to a female voice.
  4. Start the live session. Each half-second of voice is converted in about 110 ms; the picture is delayed by 0.75 s to stay in sync with the lips.

Good to know#

The Live screen tells you what is missing: no NVIDIA card, Voixbox not started, or no trained voice. Another port is set with VOIXBOX_URL in the .env.

With Avast or an antivirus that inspects connections, always go through the scripts in tools\voixbox: they remove the SSLKEYLOGFILE variable for themselves only and read Windows certificates. Otherwise Python crashes (no OPENSSL_Applink).

Grade a shot#

A seamless tile#

  2. Method: 50/50 offset with blend (natural) or 2×2 mirror (perfect, kaleidoscope effect); crop and size; optional pixel art with a shared palette.

Build a tileset#

  1. Tileset mode: click an image for material A (terrain) then a second for B (background). The Material Forge toggle takes the base colour of a Material Forge material.
  2. Choose the set: Blob 47 (eight neighbours, rounded corners) or Blob 16 (four edges, faster), the side in pixels, 1 to 5 variants per tile and the seed.
  3. Build the tileset builds the atlas and measures how neighbours join; the atlas is copied to the Library.
  4. New preview draws a random map where each cell gets the tile of its neighbours; Measure judges seams, visible repetition and lighting (ok / warning).
  5. Export atlas.png then your engine's format: Tiled (.tsx), LDtk (.ldtk) or Godot (.tres), to be placed next to the atlas.

The “Style of a bible location” block composes a tile prompt bound by a location's sheet and palette: Location prompt, then Copy prompt to paste it into the image generator. Nothing is generated here.

Prepare a part before slicing (Workbench)#

Chapter 20 showed you how to get a file out. This one shows what to do before. The Workbench screen (Game Assets → 3D Studio → 07 · Établi 3D →) is the workshop where you repair, lay down, inspect, hollow, arrange and measure a part, so the slicer only has to slice. Nothing below is irreversible: every gesture that changes the part writes one more VERSION, and all of them stay on disk — the model's life column on the left lists them. (The Workbench screen itself is in French, like the rest of the application; its labels are quoted below as they appear.)

1
Set a target size. Right rail, taille cible field: type the largest wanted dimension, in millimeters (80 for a figurine, 250 for a board). A 3D file carries no unit — while this field is empty the Workbench shows "glTF units" and refuses whatever needs a physical dimension (hollowing, arranging, exporting for print). That is deliberate: a refusal beats an invented millimeter.
2
Pick the printer. Same rail, imprimante menu: the Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2 profile is built into the application (256 × 256 mm bed, 256 mm height, one excluded area). If OrcaSlicer or ElegooSlicer is installed, its profiles show up too — read, never modified. On the plate, the green outline IS the chosen machine's bed; the red rectangle is the area where nothing may sit.
3
Repair in one click. Fiche tab → Réparer en un clic. Checked by default: coincident vertices welded, duplicate faces dropped, flat triangles dropped, normals unified. Trous bouchés (cap holes) is unchecked by default: capping adds material, tick it knowingly. The detail appears in the bottom bar — "8 retourné(s) — fermé" (8 flipped — closed). A mesh left open prints badly: better to know here than at the machine.
4
Lay the part flat. Poser sur une face (key F), then click the face that must touch the bed: the part turns and lands on the floor. The gesture joins the queue — the bottom bar says "1 modification en attente" — and écrire la version saves it. This gesture decides almost everything else: a well-placed part needs few supports. If unsure, Orienter proposes three ranked poses in the Fiche tab — share of the surface on the bed, share overhanging, height — and one click lays the part on the one you pick. A computed orientation does not always find the best pose: always review the proposal, no score knows which face you want to look good.
5
Look at the overhangs, then the slices. Surplombs button: faces leaning less than 45° from horizontal turn orange — there, a layer has almost nothing under it and the slicer will add a support. Re-lay the part until the orange recedes. Tranches button: twenty cross-sections of the part, in blue, to see its real shape at each height. Both are indicative previews: nothing is written, and the slicer does the real slicing.
6
Hollow it, when it helps. Fiche tab → Creuser, 2 mm wall to begin with: the solid inside is replaced by a shell. It is first a resin (SLA) gesture, where a solid part costs a lot of material; with filament (FDM), the slicer already fills the inside with partial infill. It needs a closed mesh (step 3), and the Workbench states the thickness that holds when the requested wall is too thick for the part. For resin, then drill a drain hole: same tab, Percer (drainage), a 3 to 4 mm diameter, then click the face to drill — preferably near the build plate, so the resin can flow out. The drill goes through the one wall under the click and stitches the hole closed; the opposite wall is left untouched. A solid part is refused (hollow it first).
7
Arrange on the bed. Sur la plaque separates the pieces, then Ranger sur le plateau lays them inside the real bed, rotated when that saves room, 2 mm apart. What does not fit moves to a second plate, and what is larger than the machine is reported. Drag a piece with the mouse (snapped to the bed grid, Shift frees it), nudge it with the arrow keys; its position, size and angle show under "Sur la plaque". The plate is a view: it is saved, but it does not change the model.
8
Measure, then export. Mesurer → two clicks give a distance, its x/y/z components and the angle between the two faces. Finally, Export tab → → Impression 3D writes the STL and the 3MF of the displayed version, at the target size in millimeters (chapter 20), and Ouvrir dans le slicer takes you there.
The order that works: repair → lay down → inspect → hollow → export. Repair first, because every other gesture starts from sound geometry — and hollowing refuses an open mesh.

Glossary#

The words your slicer will use, explained once.

TermWhat it is
Base (bed contact)The face that touches the bed. Wide and flat, the print holds; narrow, it comes loose.
OverhangA wall leaning too far to rest on the layer below. Prusa puts the clean limit without support between 45 and 60° from horizontal.
SupportA disposable structure holding overhangs up. The slicer generates it; the Workbench only helps you need less of it.
BrimA one-layer collar around the part, to keep its corners from lifting.
RaftA platform printed UNDER the whole part. Safer than a brim, costlier, and it leaves a rough face.
SkirtAn outline drawn next to the part, not touching it, to prime the flow before starting.
InfillThe pattern inside. Gyroid is about equally strong in every direction; Lightning only supports top surfaces. 15 % is usually enough.
SeamEach wall loop starts and ends somewhere, leaving a visible vertical line. The slicer places it (Nearest, Aligned, Rear) or scatters it (Random).
RetractionFilament pulled back during travel moves, to avoid strings (stringing).
LayerA horizontal slice. 0.2 mm is the usual compromise; 0.12 mm for detail, 0.28 mm for speed.
Perimeter (wall)The number of wall loops. Two perimeters at 0.45 mm make a 0.9 mm wall: the minimum to aim for when modeling.
BridgingPrinting a layer over empty space, between two supports. It works over a few centimeters, with good cooling.
WarpingCorners lifting as they cool. Remedies: clean bed, brim, closed enclosure. Common with ABS/ASA, rare with PLA.
WatertightnessA solid is watertight when its surface is closed, with no hole or free edge. That is what the Workbench bar calls "fermé" (closed).
ManifoldEvery edge belongs to exactly two faces. A mesh can be closed and still be hard to slice cleanly if it is not.
DecimationReducing the triangle count. A 500,000-triangle mesh prints no better than a 100,000 one, but slows everything down. Fiche tab → Décimer.
HollowingReplacing the solid inside with a shell of given thickness. Mostly useful in resin; 2 mm is a good starting point.
Drain holeThe hole(s) letting uncured resin (and air) out of a hollowed part. In the Workbench: Fiche tab → Percer (drainage).

3D-print your creations#

Everything the app produces in 3D — and even your vector illustrations — exports to your printer in one gesture. The circuit is designed for the Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2 (256 × 256 × 256 mm build plate) and its slicer ElegooSlicer (bundled with the machine, an OrcaSlicer fork — OrcaSlicer works too). Every export writes a folder under DeepotusVideoGenData\assets\print3d\ with three files: the STL (the mesh), the 3MF (the one you open — it carries the millimeter unit and the name), and impression.json (provenance, scale, watertightness). Everything is local and free: no provider calls.

1
Install ElegooSlicer once. The installer associates .3mf files with the slicer on Windows: the app's "Open in slicer" button uses that directly. Without an association the app tells you so, and you can set SLICER_PATH (the slicer executable path) in the data .env.
2
From the card Forge 3D (Card Forge, module 09): when the build slip delivers an STL — that is, when every element is a closed solid, the check that guarantees it — a → 3D printing button appears next to it. Leave the size empty to keep the card's real millimeters, or type a target (e.g. 80 for a figurine). These exports are marked guaranteed watertight: the producer proved it.
3
From Game Assets · 3D: every generated model (card characters, props) carries its → 3D printing button. Give the largest dimension in millimeters — 80 for a figurine, 250 for a board — and the app centers the piece and rests it on the bed. The converter reads the original model.glb; the "optimized" file (compressed for game engines) is refused with a message that says what to use instead.
4
From the Vectorlab (Export menu → 3D printing…): your vector illustrations become printable reliefs. Give a global height in millimeters, then override per layer with name=mm — for example 2, contours=5 for a stained-glass piece: glass at 2 mm, leads at 5. Strokes without a fill count by their stroke width; text objects are skipped (and counted in the message). Flat dimensions follow the document's unit (an A4 comes out at 210 × 297 mm).
5
Slice in the slicer. The .3mf opens at the right size, in millimeters, resting on the bed. Supports, fine orientation, infill, filament profiles: that's the slicer's job — the app never generates G-code. Beyond 256 mm the export warns you the slicer will have to cut or shrink, without blocking you.
Meshes coming from the image→3D engines are marked unknown watertightness in impression.json: ElegooSlicer and OrcaSlicer repair small defects on import and say so. Only the card Forge 3D guarantees its solids — its "closed solid" check states it, not a promise.
